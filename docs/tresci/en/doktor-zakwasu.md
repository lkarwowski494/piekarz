## objaw.brak-aktywnosci-w-pierwszej-dobie (RD-24-W5)
You may see nothing at all on the first day. [T:ZAK-ZAL-003]
What to do: feed the starter according to the plan for the next day. [T:-]

## objaw.brak-podwojenia (dzień 5 i później, RD-24-W3)
Possible causes: [T:-]
- A cool spot: at lower temperatures the starter's growth and fermentation slow down. [T:ZAK-SZC-004]
- A spot that is too warm: fermentation can go too fast or even stop. [T:ZAK-ZAL-008]
- White flour only: the starter may be a bit slower to get going. [T:ZAK-ZAL-010]
- Not enough time: 7 days may not be enough, and a new starter can take as long as a few weeks. [T:ZAK-ZAL-007]

What to do: [T:-]
- Keep feeding every 12 hours until the starter doubles in volume within 6–8 hours of feeding. [T:ZAK-ZAL-007]
- Put the starter in a spot at about 21 °C. [T:ZAK-ZAL-008]
- Wild yeast is more common in whole grain flour. [T:ZAK-ZAL-010]

## objaw.dlugo-bez-dokarmiania (zakwas ospały, gruba warstwa płynu)
A starter left in the fridge too long without feeding gets a thick layer of liquid and turns sluggish. [T:ZAK-LOD-004]
Possible cause: the yeast and bacteria run out of food and their numbers gradually decline. [T:ZAK-DOK-001]
What to do: feed it regularly; a run of regular feeds should bring it back. [T:ZAK-DOK-001]
Once it starts bubbling after a feeding, feed it a second time; if within 12 hours it rises and is full of bubbles, it is ready for one final feeding before use. [T:ZAK-LOD-004]
