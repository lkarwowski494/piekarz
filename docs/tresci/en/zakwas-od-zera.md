# Creating a starter from scratch, day by day (F-60, BR-ZAKW-005, RD-24) — EN
<!-- M1-audytor-generalny-104 a. EN values for the PL texts (src/lib/i18n-en/), same headings as the PL file. Role 08 implements the text verbatim (without the [T:...] marker). Discard symptoms and the per-feeding warning: see the comments in the PL file. -->

## czas.zalozenia (start)
Creating a starter takes several days of regular feeding; on average it takes 7–10 days. [T:ZAK-ZAL-001]
What tells you it's ready is doubling within 6–8 hours of a feed, not how many days have passed. [T:ZAK-ZAL-007]

## dzien.1
Mix whole grain flour (whole wheat or whole rye) with an equal weight of water, for example 113 g of flour and 113 g of water. [T:ZAK-ZAL-002]
Cover the container loosely, which makes mold less likely, and leave it for 24 hours in a warm spot, at about 21 °C. [T:ZAK-ZAL-002,ZAK-ZAL-014]

## dzien.2
You may see nothing at all on the first day; feed the starter anyway. [T:ZAK-ZAL-003]
Discard half of the starter, add flour and water in equal weights and leave it for 24 hours. [T:ZAK-ZAL-003]

## dzien.3
By day 3 you'll usually see bubbles, smell something fresh and fruity, and see it rise a little. [T:ZAK-ZAL-004]
From this day on, feed the starter twice a day, roughly every 12 hours. [T:ZAK-ZAL-004,ZAK-ZAL-012]
At each feeding, keep part of the starter, add flour and water in a 1:1:1 ratio by weight, and discard the rest. [T:ZAK-ZAL-005]
Discarding keeps the volume small, so the yeast gets more food. [T:ZAK-ZAL-013]

## dzien.4-5
By the end of day 5 the starter should at least double, be full of bubbles and smell pleasantly sour. [T:ZAK-ZAL-006]

## dzien.6-i-dalej (no doubling)
If the starter has not risen much and has few bubbles, keep feeding it every 12 hours. [T:ZAK-ZAL-007]
7 days may not be enough: a new starter can take as long as a few weeks. [T:ZAK-ZAL-007]

## gotowy
The starter is ready when it bubbles, rises and has a pleasantly sour smell. [T:ZAK-ZAL-011]
It should also clearly double within 6–8 hours after every feed. [T:ZAK-ZAL-007]
Only put it in the fridge once it has become established, after 7–10 days. [T:ZAK-ZAL-012]

## temperatura
A starter grows best at a warm room temperature, about 21 °C. [T:ZAK-ZAL-008]
In the cold it grows more slowly, and when it is too warm fermentation can go too fast or even stop. [T:ZAK-SZC-004,ZAK-ZAL-008]
If your home is usually below 20 °C, find the starter a warmer spot. [T:ZAK-ZAL-009]

## maka
Wild yeast is more common in whole grain flour, and a starter made with white flour only may be a bit slower to get going. [T:ZAK-ZAL-010]

## odrzucenie (link to the starter check)
If you see a film, spots or discoloration, or notice a rotten smell, open the starter check. [T:-]
