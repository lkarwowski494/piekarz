## OVERPROOFED_COLLAPSE.titlePl
Over-proofed dough [T:-]

## OVERPROOFED_COLLAPSE.triggerSummaryPl
According to Modernist Cuisine, when dough is over-proofed, the dent from gently pressing it with a finger stays. [T:RAT-PRZ-001]

## OVERPROOFED_COLLAPSE.immediateActionPl
Reshape yeast dough: according to King Arthur Baking, a badly over-proofed yeast loaf baked right away collapses in the oven, though it still tastes good. [T:RAT-PRZ-002]

## OVERPROOFED_COLLAPSE.stepByStepPl[0]
Sourdough usually can't be saved this way: according to King Arthur Baking this method generally doesn't work, and according to Modernist Cuisine it's worth trying only if the dough was made and proofed within a few hours. [T:RAT-PRZ-004,RAT-PRZ-005]

## OVERPROOFED_COLLAPSE.stepByStepPl[1]
Sourdough proofed overnight in the cold is tough after degassing: according to Modernist Cuisine the loaf will rise poorly, be misshapen and very sour. [T:RAT-PRZ-004]

## OVERPROOFED_COLLAPSE.stepByStepPl[2]
If you're saving the dough, take it out of the basket or bowl. [T:RAT-PRZ-003]

## OVERPROOFED_COLLAPSE.stepByStepPl[3]
Degas it by pressing down firmly, as you do when shaping. [T:RAT-PRZ-003]

## OVERPROOFED_COLLAPSE.stepByStepPl[4]
Shape it again and let it proof. [T:RAT-PRZ-003]

## OVERPROOFED_COLLAPSE.scientificReasoningPl
According to Modernist Cuisine, over-proofed dough collapses because its gluten structure is weakened and it has produced too much gas. [T:RAT-PRZ-006]

## SLACK_HIGH_HYDRATION.titlePl
Dough too slack [T:-]

## SLACK_HIGH_HYDRATION.triggerSummaryPl
The dough is sticky and loose and won't hold its shape. [T:-]

## SLACK_HIGH_HYDRATION.immediateActionPl
Check that the salt is in the dough: according to King Arthur Baking, dough without salt is sticky and loose, because salt absorbs moisture and tightens gluten. [T:RAT-SOL-001]

## SLACK_HIGH_HYDRATION.stepByStepPl[0]
If the salt is missing, open the "Forgotten salt" protocol. [T:-]

## SLACK_HIGH_HYDRATION.stepByStepPl[1]
Stretch and fold the dough during fermentation: according to King Arthur Baking this helps develop the gluten network and strengthens the dough. [T:KRO-POC-006]

## SLACK_HIGH_HYDRATION.stepByStepPl[2]
According to King Arthur Baking, for bread made with 100% rye flour, folding can usually be skipped. [T:KRO-POC-006]

## SLACK_HIGH_HYDRATION.scientificReasoningPl
According to King Arthur Baking, folding during fermentation helps develop the gluten network and strengthens the dough, and salt tightens gluten. [T:KRO-POC-006,RAT-SOL-001]

## COLD_SLUGGISH.titlePl
Sluggish fermentation (Cold kitchen) [T:-]

## COLD_SLUGGISH.triggerSummaryPl
The dough is rising more slowly than usual: according to King Arthur Baking, a common cause is the cool spot where it sits. [T:RAT-ZIM-001]

## COLD_SLUGGISH.immediateActionPl
Move the dough somewhere warmer: according to King Arthur Baking, yeast grows best at about 24–25.5 °C. [T:RAT-ZIM-002]

## COLD_SLUGGISH.stepByStepPl[2]
Other warm spots, according to King Arthur Baking: the top of the refrigerator or a high shelf, because heat rises. [T:RAT-ZIM-003]

## COLD_SLUGGISH.stepByStepPl[3]
Cover the bowl with a lid or plastic wrap: according to King Arthur Baking, moisture keeps the skin of the dough soft and helps it rise. [T:RAT-ZIM-004]

## COLD_SLUGGISH.stepByStepPl[4]
Judge the dough by how it looks, not by the clock: according to King Arthur Baking, dough takes longer to rise in cold air, and recipe times are only a guideline. [T:RAT-ZIM-005]

## COLD_SLUGGISH.scientificReasoningPl
In the range 20–26 °C, Baker uses an approximation from a research model: 6 °C cooler (for example 20 instead of 26 °C) means about 2 times longer fermentation. [T:FER-Q10-002]

## FORGOTTEN_SALT.titlePl
Forgotten salt [T:-]

## FORGOTTEN_SALT.triggerSummaryPl
The dough is already mixed, and the weighed salt is still on the counter. [T:-]

## FORGOTTEN_SALT.immediateActionPl
Add the salt before the first rise: according to King Arthur Baking, yeast dough without salt rises faster than usual, has less flavor and a weaker structure. [T:RAT-SOL-002,RAT-SOL-005]

## FORGOTTEN_SALT.stepByStepPl[0]
Don't taste the dough to check the salt: according to the FDA, raw dough should not be eaten or tasted. [T:BEZ-SUR-001]

## FORGOTTEN_SALT.stepByStepPl[1]
Mix the salt with a few teaspoons of water. [T:RAT-SOL-003]

## FORGOTTEN_SALT.stepByStepPl[2]
Press the dough out flat, spread the mixture over it, roll it up, press it out again, rotate it 90° and roll it up again. [T:RAT-SOL-003]

## FORGOTTEN_SALT.stepByStepPl[3]
Knead until the dough is fairly smooth again. [T:RAT-SOL-003]

## FORGOTTEN_SALT.stepByStepPl[4]
With sourdough, according to King Arthur Baking, you can treat the pause as an autolyse: after a 20–60 minute rest, mix in the salt and continue with the recipe. [T:RAT-SOL-004]

## FORGOTTEN_SALT.stepByStepPl[5]
If you notice the missing salt only after the first rise, according to King Arthur Baking adding it won't help and re-kneading can permanently damage the dough: bake the bread without salt or use the dough for a pizza crust. [T:RAT-SOL-005]

## FORGOTTEN_SALT.scientificReasoningPl
According to King Arthur Baking, salt slows fermentation, absorbs moisture and tightens gluten. [T:RAT-SOL-001,RAT-SOL-002]
