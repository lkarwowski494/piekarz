## starter_feed (also rye_starter)
The yeast and bacteria in a starter need regular feeding with fresh flour and water to survive and grow. [T:KRO-POC-001]
According to King Arthur Baking, a starter is ready for baking when it doubles in volume within 6–8 hours of feeding and is full of bubbles. [T:KRO-POC-002]

## autolyse
According to King Arthur Baking, resting flour and water before adding salt lets the flour absorb the water, starts gluten formation and makes kneading easier. [T:KRO-POC-003]

## mix (not rye_mix)
According to King Arthur Baking, kneading develops the gluten, so the dough traps the gas made by the yeast and rises. [T:KRO-POC-004]

## bulk_ferment (not rye_rest)
According to King Arthur Baking, fermentation produces acids, which give the dough flavour and strength, and carbon dioxide, which gives it volume and lightness. [T:KRO-POC-005]
Folding helps develop the gluten network and strengthens the dough. [T:KRO-POC-006]

## pre_shape
According to King Arthur Baking, pre-shaping gives the dough the basic shape of the loaf without refining it yet. [T:KRO-POC-007]

## bench_rest
According to King Arthur Baking, every time you handle dough the gluten tightens, and a rest relaxes it, so final shaping is easier. [T:KRO-POC-008]

## final_shape (not rye_shape)
According to King Arthur Baking, the basket supports and shapes the rising loaf. [T:KRO-POC-009]

## final_proof (not rye_proof)
According to King Arthur Baking, this is the dough's final rise before baking. [T:KRO-POC-010]
The cold of the fridge slows fermentation, and according to the same source slower fermentation deepens the flavour. [T:KRO-POC-010]

## preheat_oven
According to King Arthur Baking, the pot is preheated empty so that steam forms inside it when the dough goes in. [T:KRO-POC-011]

## bake (not rye_bake)
According to King Arthur Baking, the slash gives the loaf a place to expand, so the bread keeps its shape and does not burst at the side. [T:KRO-POC-012]

## lid_on
According to King Arthur Baking, the steam under the lid keeps the crust soft longer, so the loaf can keep rising early in the bake. [T:KRO-POC-013]

## lid_off
According to King Arthur Baking, without the lid the bread keeps baking until it is fully browned. [T:KRO-POC-014]

## cool
According to King Arthur Baking, bread releases moisture as it cools, and the crumb needs that time to set. [T:KRO-POC-015]
Sliced too early, it can be gummy. [T:KRO-POC-015]

## rye_cool
According to King Arthur Baking, bread releases moisture as it cools, and the crumb needs that time to set. [T:KRO-POC-015]
Moist, dense breads with a high share of rye often benefit from cooling for up to 24 hours. [T:KRO-POC-016]
