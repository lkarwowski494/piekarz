## starter_feed (also rye_starter)
The yeast and bacteria in a starter need regular feeding with fresh flour and water to survive and grow. [T:KRO-POC-001]
Your starter is ready to bake with when it doubles within 6–8 hours of a feed and is full of bubbles. [T:KRO-POC-002]

## autolyse
Resting the flour and water before adding salt lets the flour soak up the water, gets the gluten started and makes kneading easier. [T:KRO-POC-003]

## mix (not rye_mix)
Kneading develops the gluten, so the dough holds on to the gas from the yeast and rises. [T:KRO-POC-004]

## bulk_ferment (not rye_rest)
Fermentation makes acids, which give the dough flavour and strength, and carbon dioxide, which gives it volume and lightness. [T:KRO-POC-005]
Folding helps develop the gluten network and strengthens the dough. [T:KRO-POC-006]

## pre_shape
Pre-shaping gives the dough a rough loaf shape, before the finishing touches. [T:KRO-POC-007]

## bench_rest
Every time you handle the dough the gluten tightens, and a rest relaxes it, so final shaping goes more easily. [T:KRO-POC-008]

## final_shape (not rye_shape)
The basket supports the rising loaf and gives it its shape. [T:KRO-POC-009]

## final_proof (not rye_proof)
This is the dough's last rise before baking. [T:KRO-POC-010]
The cold of the fridge slows fermentation, and slower fermentation means deeper flavour. [T:KRO-POC-010]

## preheat_oven
You preheat the pot empty so steam builds up inside once the dough goes in. [T:KRO-POC-011]

## bake (not rye_bake)
The slash gives the loaf a place to open up, so it keeps its shape and doesn't burst at the side. [T:KRO-POC-012]

## lid_on
Steam under the lid keeps the crust soft for longer, so the loaf can keep rising early in the bake. [T:KRO-POC-013]

## lid_off
With the lid off, the bread keeps baking until it's fully browned. [T:KRO-POC-014]

## cool
Bread gives off moisture as it cools, and the crumb needs that time to set. [T:KRO-POC-015]
Sliced too early, it can be gummy. [T:KRO-POC-015]

## rye_cool
Bread gives off moisture as it cools, and the crumb needs that time to set. [T:KRO-POC-015]
Moist, dense breads with a high share of rye often benefit from cooling for up to 24 hours. [T:KRO-POC-016]
